## Slide 5

Why Data Matters
Data is essential for effective leadership in contact centers. It provides crucial insights into agent performance, patient satisfaction, and operational efficiency. By closely monitoring key performance indicators (KPIs), leaders can identify trends, diagnose issues, and make informed decisions to drive continuous improvement.

## Slide 6

Core KPIs Every Leader Must Understand
KPI
Definition
Formula
AHT (Average Handle Time)
The average time an agent spends handling a patient interaction, including talk time, hold time, and after-call work.
Total talk time + hold time + after-call work / Total calls
CSAT (patient Satisfaction)
A measure of patient satisfaction, typically collected through a survey after an interaction.
Number of satisfied patients / Total survey responses
FCR (First Call Resolution)
The percentage of patient inquiries that are resolved on the first contact, without requiring a follow-up.
Number of issues resolved on the first call / Total calls
NPS (Net Promoter Score)
A patient loyalty metric that measures how likely patients are to recommend a product or service to others.
Number of promoters - Number of detractors / Total survey responses
SLA (Service Level Agreement)
The percentage of calls answered within a specified time frame, typically 20 or 30 seconds.
Calls answered within SLA / Total calls
Occupancy
The percentage of time agents spend handling calls or work, versus being idle.
Total talk time + hold time + after-call work / Total logged-in time
Adherence
The measure of how closely agents follow their scheduled shifts and breaks.
Total time worked as scheduled / Total scheduled time
*Data sourced from industry standards and best practices for contact center key performance indicators.

Root Cause Analysis Tools
5 Whys
A technique to identify the root cause of a problem by repeatedly asking 'Why?' to peel away the layers of symptoms and get to the core issue.
Fishbone Diagram
A cause-and-effect diagram that helps identify all the potential or real causes that contribute to an effect or problem. (People, Process, tech, KPIs, Standards)
These root cause analysis tools help uncover the underlying factors driving problems, allowing organizations to implement targeted solutions and drive continuous improvement.

Pitfalls of Data Analysis

## Slide 17

Examples of Flawed Analysis
Mistaking correlation for causation Avoid assuming that just because two variables are correlated, one is causing the other. Jumping to conclusions after one data point Resist the temptation to draw firm conclusions based on a single data point, look for trends over time. Ignoring sample size Ensure the data sample is large enough to be statistically significant and representative of the overall population.
Confirmation bias Be aware of the tendency to interpret information in a way that confirms your preexisting beliefs. Recency bias Avoid overemphasizing the most recent data and neglecting older but still relevant information. Overreliance on averages Understand that averages can mask important variations and outliers in your data.

Biases That Distort Data
Confirmation Bias
The tendency to interpret information in a way that confirms your existing beliefs
Recency Bias
Overemphasizing the most recent data and neglecting older but relevant information
Overreliance on Averages
Averages can mask important variations and outliers in the data
Being aware of these common biases can help contact center leaders avoid flawed analysis and make more informed, data-driven decisions.
