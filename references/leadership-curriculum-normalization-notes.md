## Module 1 curriculum structure

The first deck is a leadership data-analysis module organized into five instructional bands: reading and understanding KPIs, translating data into actionable insights, pitfalls of data analysis, tools and resources for analysis, and real-world examples of effective data analysis. The reusable concepts are KPI literacy, trend reading, target comparison, outlier detection, moving from data to insight to action, root-cause analysis using 5 Whys and fishbone, validating conclusions across time periods and teams, and building repeatable dashboard-review habits.

The strongest reusable LMS activities are the KPI interpretation activity, the CSAT-drop scenario, the insight-to-action activity, the simple report-view activity, the story-analysis exercise, the reflection prompt, and the practical follow-through prompt about setting one data-driven goal and scheduling recurring KPI reviews.

## Module 2 curriculum structure

The second deck is organized around five coaching pillars, coaching preparation and delivery, documenting goals, tailoring training to learner style, and accountability conversations. The reusable concepts are trust-building, self-discovery, commitment creation, goal execution, accountability, SMART goals, behavior-based feedback, discovery questions, coaching documentation, learning-style adaptation, discerning skill versus will, owning the message, difficult conversations, and corrective-action follow-through.

The strongest reusable LMS activities are SMART-goal writing, learning-style identification, difficult-conversation practice, and corrective-action follow-up framing. The deck also includes explicit CH Consulting Group references and contact slides that must not surface in the learner experience.

## Module 3 curriculum structure

The third deck is a performance-management module built around performance buckets, bias avoidance, objective calibration, QA-driven coaching, high-performer development, coaching the movable middle, low-performance intervention, performance-improvement plans, adherence coaching, daily leadership rituals, and reflective planning.

The strongest reusable LMS activities are bucketing activity, score-this-call calibration practice, tailored performance-plan design, adherence coaching, and reflection on what to change tomorrow. One slide contains a clear placeholder — "INSERT CLIENT QA FORM" — which should be replaced with neutral LMS-native scoring and calibration content rather than mirrored literally.

## Module 4 curriculum structure

The fourth deck focuses on remote-team engagement through gamification. Its structure includes work-at-home leadership strategies, WAH operating rhythm, gamification foundations, engagement psychology, program design, rewards, common mistakes, activity design, balancing competition and collaboration, recognition in remote teams, workshop design, measuring engagement, survey and pulse-check logic, adjustment loops, and reflection.

The strongest reusable LMS activities are program-design steps, reward design, game ideation, weekly recognition cycle design, engagement measurement, pulse-check review, start-stop-change adjustment, and next-7-day reflection planning.

## Branding and watermark cleanup targets

The uploaded decks contain client-specific or source-specific language that should be removed from the LMS experience. This includes the CH Consulting Group references in Module 2, the contact slide and team slide in Module 2, the repeated confidential footer and CH Consulting Group watermark text in Module 4, and the client placeholder slide in Module 3. The LMS should keep the instructional content but strip proprietary footer language, speaker/contact information, and any explicit "insert client" placeholders.

## Naming normalization targets inside the app

The app currently contains inconsistent naming that should be normalized to the deck titles. The highest-priority corrections are: change "Unlocking the power of date" to **Unlocking the Power of Data**; change "Real Time Coaching" to **Real-time Coaching**; change shorthand performance titles to **Utilizing Performance Management to Maximize Results** where they represent the uploaded module; and change "Gamification & Work From Home" to **Gamification for Remote Teams: Engaging and Empowering Leaders**.

The supporting module titles and summaries should also match the curriculum language more closely. For example, leadership library assets should describe KPI interpretation, root-cause analysis, SMART coaching, performance buckets, bias-aware calibration, gamification design, recognition loops, and remote-team engagement using the deck terminology rather than internal shorthand labels.
